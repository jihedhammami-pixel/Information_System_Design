Exigences fonctionnelles et non fonctionnelles

Une exigence dit ce qu’un système doit faire, ou avec quelle qualité. Si elles sont fausses, l’équipe construit la mauvaise chose, aussi bon que soit le code. Les chapitres vont du plus simple au plus difficile : les étiquettes de couleur indiquent le niveau.

Qu’est-ce qu’une exigence ? DÉBUTANT

Un besoin que le système doit satisfaire

"A condition or capability needed by a user to solve a problem or achieve an objective" and "a condition or capability that must be met or possessed by a system … to satisfy a contract, standard, specification, or other formally imposed document."IEEE Std 610.12-1990, Standard Glossary of Software Engineering Terminology

Requirements come in layers. Business requirements explain why the system exists. User requirements say what users need to do. Functional requirements say what the system must do, and non-functional requirements say how well it must do it. Click a layer of the 3D cake, or a button.

Fonctionnelle vs non fonctionnelle DÉBUTANT

« Ce qu’il fait » vs « avec quelle qualité »

✅ Functional requirement (FR)

A behaviour or function: an input, an action, an output. "The system shall let a member reserve a book." If it is missing, a user can't do something.

⭐ Non-functional requirement (NFR)

A quality or a constraint on the whole system or a function: speed, security, availability, usability, legal rules. "95% of searches shall return in under 2 seconds." If it is missing, users can do the task, but badly.

🔒 Constraints

Limits on the solution: technology, law, budget, standards. "Personal data shall be stored only in the EU." Usually listed with the NFRs.

Test rapide. Ask: "Does this describe something the system does for someone?" → FR. "Does it describe a quality (how fast, how secure, how easy) or a limit?" → NFR. NFRs are also called quality attributes or "-ilities".

Types d’ENF : le modèle de qualité ISO 25010 INTERMÉDIAIRE

Neuf caractéristiques de qualité produit dans l’édition 2023

Instead of inventing categories, use a standard checklist. ISO/IEC 25010:2023 defines nine product quality characteristics, each with sub-characteristics. Turn the 3D carousel to explore them. Sub-characteristics with a gold border are new or renamed in 2023.

What changed from ISO 25010:2011?

Usability became interaction capability, portability became flexibility (now with scalability), and safety was added as a ninth characteristic.

Another classic: FURPS+

From Hewlett-Packard (Robert Grady): Functionality, Usability, Reliability, Performance, Supportability, and + for constraints (design, implementation, interface, physical).

Rédiger de bonnes exigences INTERMÉDIAIRE

Assez claires pour que deux personnes les lisent pareil, et qu’un testeur puisse les vérifier

Use "shall" for obligations

"Shall" is binding. "Should" and "may" read as optional.✗ The system should send a receipt.✓ The system shall email a receipt after each payment.

One requirement per statement

A tester must be able to pass or fail it as one unit.✗ … shall log in users and export reports.✓ two separate requirements

Verifiable, with numbers

Replace adjectives with measures.✗ The search shall be fast.✓ 95% of searches shall return within 2 s.

Say what, not how

Leave design choices to designers, unless it is a real constraint.✗ … shall use a MySQL table for loans.✓ … shall keep the loan history for 5 years.

ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 lists the characteristics of a well-formed requirement: necessary, appropriate, unambiguous, complete, singular, feasible, verifiable, correct, conforming.

    User stories et critères d’acceptation EXPERT

    La façon agile d’exprimer les exigences fonctionnelles

    As a library member, I want to reserve a book that is on loan, so that I get it as soon as it is returned.

    A story is a placeholder for a conversation, not a full specification. The details live in its acceptance criteria, often written in Gherkin (Given / When / Then), which can become automated tests.

    Good stories are INVEST (Bill Wake): Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable.

    Scenario: Reserve a book that is on loan Given the book "Dune" is on loan until 12 May And I have fewer than 5 active reservations When I reserve "Dune" Then my reservation is confirmed And I am notified by email when "Dune" is returned
    Where do NFRs go? Not in one story. Write them as constraints that every story must respect, often as part of the team's "Definition of Done" (e.g. "every page meets WCAG 2.2 AA").

    Les exigences sur un seul écran

    FR

    What the system does: "The system shall <action> <object> <condition>."

    NFR

    How well it does it, or limits on it: performance, security, availability, usability, legal.

    ISO 25010:2023

    Functional suitability · Performance efficiency · Compatibility · Interaction capability · Reliability · Security · Maintainability · Flexibility · Safety.

    Good requirement

    Necessary, unambiguous, singular, feasible, verifiable, correct. Use "shall", one per sentence, numbers not adjectives.

    EARS

    Ubiquitous · When (event) · While (state) · If…then (unwanted) · Where (feature).

    Measurable NFR

    Source · stimulus · environment · artifact · response · response measure. Use percentiles.

    Les bases DÉBUTANT

    Qui utilise le système, et pour quoi faire ?

    A use case is a goal an actor achieves with the system, producing a result of value: "Borrow book", "Pay fine". Ivar Jacobson introduced use cases in 1987, and UML adopted them. A use case diagram gives an overview of all of them. It does not show order, screens or data. Flip the cards for each element.

    Règle de nommage. Name a use case with a verb + noun from the actor's point of view: "Borrow book", not "Book" or "Loan processing". Name actors with a role: "Member", not "Sara".

    Les acteurs en détail INTERMÉDIAIRE

    Un rôle joué par quelqu’un ou quelque chose d’extérieur au système

    👤 Primary actor

    Starts the use case to reach a goal. Member starts "Borrow book". Usually drawn on the left.

    🖥️ Supporting (secondary) actor

    The system needs its help: a payment service, an email server. Usually drawn on the right.

    ⏰ Time as an actor

    Scheduled jobs ("every night at 02:00, send reminders") are often shown with a Clock or Timer actor.

    🎭 Role, not person

    One person can play several roles (a librarian who borrows a book is a Member then). One role can be many people.

    Descriptions de cas d’utilisation AVANCÉ

    Le diagramme est la table des matières. Le texte est le contenu.

    Each use case gets a written description. Alistair Cockburn (Writing Effective Use Cases, 2000) describes three formats: brief (one paragraph), casual (a few paragraphs) and fully dressed (the template below). He also defines goal levels:

    ☁️Summary

    A business goal over many sessions: "Run the library".

    🌊User goal ("sea level")

    One sitting, one actor, one goal: "Borrow book". Most use cases belong here.

    🐟Subfunction

    A piece of a user goal: "Identify member". Often an «include».

    Repérer les erreurs AVANCÉ

    Ce diagramme de « boutique en ligne » contient 6 erreurs. Cliquez sur les éléments qui vous semblent faux.

      Bonnes pratiques

      No functional decomposition

      Use cases are goals, not menu items or steps.✗ Click "Buy" · Enter address · Validate card✓ Buy products

      Don't draw a flowchart

      Use case diagrams have no order. Lines between use cases are only include, extend or generalization.✗ Browse → Add to cart → Checkout✓ an activity diagram for the flow

      Use «include» and «extend» sparingly

      They help with shared or optional behaviour. Too many make the diagram unreadable.✗ 15 include arrows on one diagram✓ a few, where text would repeat

      Actors stay outside

      Everything inside the boundary is the system you build.✗ Database inside, as an actor✓ external systems outside, as actors

      Des exigences aux cas d’utilisation EXPERT

      Relier la fenêtre 1 à la fenêtre 2

      Functional requirements group into use cases: one use case usually covers several FRs (its steps and alternatives). Non-functional requirements are not use cases. Attach them as notes, write them in the use case's "special requirements" section, or keep them in a supplementary specification. A traceability link from each requirement to its use case lets you check that nothing is forgotten. Click a requirement or a use case.

      Use cases in UML 2.5

      The subject (the boundary) can be any classifier: a system, a subsystem or a component. Use cases can be owned by a classifier, and can have their own behaviour diagrams (activity, sequence, state machine).

      Extension points

      Written in a compartment of the base use case. The «extend» can carry a condition in a note; if no condition is given, the extension always applies.

      Abstract use cases and actors

      A use case that is never run on its own (only through specialization or inclusion) can be marked abstract, with its name in italics.

      Cheat sheet

      Les diagrammes de cas d’utilisation sur un seul écran

      Elements

      Actor (stick figure or «actor» box) · use case (ellipse, verb + noun) · subject (rectangle with the system name).

      Lines

      Association: solid line actor–use case. Generalization: solid line, hollow triangle at the parent.

      «include» / «extend»

      Dashed arrows. Include: base → included, always. Extend: extension → base, conditional, at an extension point.

      Descriptions

      Actors, preconditions, trigger, main success scenario, extensions, postconditions (guarantees).
